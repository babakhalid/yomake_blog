Adobe announced Adobe Firefly Builder on November 10, 2026, at its MAX conference in Miami Beach. You describe a website, presentation or app in plain language and get an interactive prototype you can edit on a canvas. It is in private beta, behind a waitlist, and Adobe has announced no price for it.

A prototype shows an idea. It does not take a booking or a payment, and it does not sit on your domain. If you need the published version this week, [Youmake](https://www.youmake.dev/) builds websites, web apps, mobile apps and slide decks from a description and publishes them to a domain you own. This article covers what Adobe confirmed, what it left open, and what to do while the waitlist moves.

## What is Adobe Firefly Builder?

Adobe's press release describes Firefly Builder this way: it "helps creators transform ideas into interactive, on-brand prototypes, like a website, presentation or app, using natural language, with no code required for the initial prototype."

Adobe has confirmed three things about how it works:

- You prompt first and edit by hand after. Once the first prototype exists, the release says, "creators then edit any element directly on the canvas." You do not have to write a new prompt to move a button.
- A design system "keeps the project consistent" as you iterate.
- You can try several versions at once. Adobe's blog post lists "the ability to explore multiple creative directions in parallel," which means comparing two layouts without overwriting one with the other.

Builder arrived alongside two other new Firefly tools, both in beta: Firefly Design, a single canvas for brand and campaign work, and Firefly Graph, a node-based workflow builder. InsideAI News called Builder "a direct challenge to Figma's dominance in product design," and MakeUseOf's headline said Adobe is "taking vibe coding beyond apps."

If the term is new to you, our explainer on [what vibe coding is](/articles/what-is-vibe-coding) covers the basics.

## What Adobe has not said yet

I read the press release and the Adobe blog post for anything about shipping. Neither mentions:

- publishing to a custom domain, or hosting of any kind
- exporting the code or syncing it to GitHub
- a database, user logins or payments
- submitting a mobile app to an app store
- a price or a credit cost for Builder

Adobe may add some of these later, or may already have them in the beta without saying so. I do not know, and nobody outside the beta does either. What the announcement does establish is the word Adobe chose for the output, which is "prototype," in both documents.

So plan around what is confirmed. Firefly Builder is a design tool that produces something clickable to show a client or a team. It has no public date, and nothing Adobe published says it will get your business online.

## What this changes for people who build with AI

Once designers who already pay for Creative Cloud get a prompt box that produces a clickable website, two things follow for freelancers, founders and small agencies.

**Clients will expect to see something clickable early.** A static mockup in a pitch will look slow next to a competitor who shows a working flow in the first meeting. That was already true for anyone using an [AI app builder](/articles/best-ai-app-builders). Adobe's entry will spread the habit to designers who have never used one.

**The prototype becomes the cheap part.** If anyone can generate a convincing first screen, the work clients pay for moves to what comes after: real content, a form that sends email, a login, a payment, a domain, a hand-off. I think this matters more than the launch itself. A tool that stops at the prototype leaves that work to somebody else, and the client is paying for that work.

There is a practical trap here too. A prototype in one tool and a live build in another means building the thing twice. Before you start in any tool, check whether it can carry the project to launch. Our [AI web app builder checklist](/articles/ai-web-app-builder) lists six checks for that, and the guide to [AI app builders that let you export your code](/articles/ai-app-builder-code-export) explains why ownership should be on the list.

## What to do now

1. **Join the waitlist if you live in Adobe's tools.** If your brand assets are already in Creative Cloud, Builder may fit how you explore ideas. Adobe links the waitlist from its press release.
2. **Do not schedule client work around a private beta.** There is no public date. A launch that waits on an invitation email has no deadline you control.
3. **Decide what you need: a picture of the product, or the product.** For an internal pitch, a prototype is enough. For a restaurant that needs bookings by December, it is not.
4. **Write the brief once.** Pages, sections, audience, tone, and the one action a visitor should take. That brief works as a prompt in any tool, so the time is not wasted whichever way you go.
5. **Build the live version now.** The steps are below.

Only by invitation. Adobe announced it on November 10, 2026, as a private beta with a waitlist. Adobe has not published a date for a public release.

Adobe did not announce a price or a credit cost for Builder. The press release points to the general Firefly plans page and says only that the new tools are in beta.
