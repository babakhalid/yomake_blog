AI freelance projects on Upwork carry fixed-price budgets about 52% higher than comparable non-AI projects, according to a report OpenAI and Upwork published on November 10, 2026. The posted hourly rate for the same kind of work is only about 1.5% higher. The extra money is in the size of the project: clients are buying bigger jobs that end in something finished. If you freelance, that changes what you should sell. ## What the OpenAI and Upwork report found

The report is called "The Rebundling of Freelance Work in the AI Age." Six researchers from OpenAI and Upwork compared more than 36,000 sampled ChatGPT conversations with Upwork job postings, then looked at how clients combine tasks inside a single posting.

| Measure | AI-related postings vs non-AI postings |
|---|---|
| Fixed-price budget | About 70% higher raw, about 52% higher after controlling for work subcategory and posting month |
| Posted hourly rate | About 9% higher raw, about 1.5% higher after the same controls |
| Hours worked in the first 90 days of a hired hourly contract | About 41% higher |
| Distinct tasks per posting | 3.35 vs 3.19 |
| Share of task pairs that were rare before generative AI | 8.4% vs 2.4% |

The report also found:

- People use ChatGPT mostly for writing and advice. Content writing is 14.5% of the sampled ChatGPT conversations and about 1.3% of Upwork job posts. Over two years, content-writing postings fell at more than twice the rate of Upwork postings overall.
- Upwork demand leans toward technical implementation and visual production: graphic and presentation design, video and animation, QA testing, mobile development, digital marketing and web development. Video and animation postings grew by about a third relative to the marketplace.
- Of 63 matched work subcategories, 43 fit one pattern: activities common in ChatGPT lost Upwork share, and activities less common there gained it.
- Inside AI-related postings, the work is spreading out. Building chatbots and machine-learning models is still the largest task cluster, but its share fell from 21.8% in 2023 to 17.8% in 2025. Video production, social media campaign management, marketing automation and AI API integration gained share.

## Why bigger budgets with a flat hourly rate matter

A 52% budget premium next to a 1.5% hourly premium means clients do not pay much more for an hour of "AI work." They post larger projects and buy more hours. The authors say larger AI-related budgets "may therefore reflect larger or more intensive projects rather than simply a higher price for an otherwise identical unit of labor."

So adding "AI" to your profile headline will not raise your rate. Selling a wider scope might raise your invoice.

The second lesson is about which piece of your work a client still needs you for. A client who can draft a blog post or get basic advice in a chat window has less reason to hire for that task alone. They still hire for implementation, production and delivery. The report's conclusion puts it this way: "Value may increasingly lie in defining the problem, connecting tools and activities, applying domain judgment, and delivering a finished outcome."

## What to do now: sell a finished outcome

1. Find the part of your service that clients now do themselves. First drafts and generic advice are the obvious candidates. Stop selling them alone.
2. Attach the implementation. A copywriter can sell a landing page that is live with the copy in it. A marketer can sell the campaign plus the page it sends people to. A consultant can sell the audit plus a small internal tool that tracks the fixes.
3. Price by scope, with a fixed fee. The premium in the data sits in fixed-price budgets. Write down what the client gets, by when, and for how much.
4. Pair one AI skill with one traditional skill. The report's own examples are AI-generated video combined with editing and motion graphics, and chatbot development combined with application integration. In its appendix, pairs that mix an AI task with a traditional one are the largest group among the new combinations.
5. Put the offer on a page you own. A marketplace profile lists skills. A page lets you describe one outcome, its scope, its price and an example.

## Honest limits

- The data covers Upwork postings and a sample of ChatGPT conversations. The authors write that neither "represents the labor market as a whole" and treat the findings as descriptive. They do not claim AI caused the changes.
- Budgets and hourly rates are what clients posted. The report does not measure what freelancers earned.
- OpenAI and Upwork both have a commercial interest in how this story is told. Read it as marketplace evidence from two interested parties.
- Bigger budgets come with more work. Hired hourly contracts logged about 41% more hours, so a wider scope raises your invoice and your workload together.

## FAQ

**Do AI freelance projects pay more per hour?**
Barely, in this data. Posted hourly rates were about 1.5% higher for AI-related postings after controls. Fixed-price budgets were about 52% higher, and hired hourly contracts ran about 41% more hours in the first 90 days.

**Which freelance services are losing demand to AI?**
The report singles out content writing: it is common in ChatGPT use, and its Upwork postings fell at more than twice the rate of postings overall. Within AI-related postings, AI-generated written content and training programs also lost share.
