## What is a client portal, and who needs one?

A client portal is a login-protected web app where one client sees only their own material. Typical contents:

- A project status page with milestones and next steps
- A shared files area for briefs, drafts and final deliverables
- An approvals list ("approve this design", "confirm this scope change")
- Invoices or a link to pay them
- A message thread, so decisions live in one place

You need one when you handle more than a few clients at once and keep answering "where is that file?" or "what is the status?". Freelancers use portals to look more established and to cut status emails. Agencies use them to keep many clients organized under one brand. ## What should the first version include?

Resist the urge to copy a full enterprise portal. Pick the three jobs that cost you the most email today and build only those. For a designer that is usually file delivery, approvals and a status view. For a consultant it may be a shared action list and a monthly report. For a small agency it is often a single page per client that shows what is in progress, what is waiting on the client, and what is done.

Write those jobs down before you open the builder, then turn each into one sentence of your prompt. Show the portal to one friendly client first, watch where they hesitate, and fix that before you invite anyone else. A portal that three clients use weekly beats a feature-rich one nobody logs into.

## Honest limits

A portal holds client files and sometimes payment details, so treat it seriously.

- **Test access rules yourself.** The security scan helps, but only you can confirm that each client sees only their data.
- **Start small.** Status, files and approvals cover most needs. Add invoicing and messaging after real clients use it.
- **Credits are used per change.** Concise prompts cost less. Pricing is on the Youmake site: there is no free plan, and the trial is $1 for 3 days.
- **Compliance is on you.** If you store sensitive client data, check the rules that apply to your industry and region.

## FAQ

**What is the best way to build a client portal without coding?**
Describe the portal in plain English to an AI app builder, test it with a second account, and publish to your own domain. Keep the first version to status, files and approvals.

**Can I white label a client portal I build with Youmake?**
You publish to your own domain and control the design, so clients see your brand. You can also export the code or sync it to GitHub for hand-off.

**How long does it take to build a client portal?**
A basic version often takes an afternoon. Testing access rules and polishing the design usually takes longer than the first build.
