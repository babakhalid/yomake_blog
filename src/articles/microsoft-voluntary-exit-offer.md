On October 26, 2026, Microsoft told employees on select teams that they can take a Voluntary Exit Offer. Euronews reports that about 60,000 people are eligible. If you are one of them, or you work somewhere that could follow, the useful question is what you will have ready by the time you have to decide. With [Youmake](https://www.youmake.dev/ai-website-builder), you can have a consulting site, a portfolio or a small client portal live in a few days, before the decision, not after.

## What Microsoft announced

Chief People Officer Amy Coleman published the announcement on [Microsoft's blog](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/10/26/introducing-a-voluntary-exit-offer/). The facts that appear there:

- It is a "time-limited, voluntary program" offering "financial support and career planning resources."
- It applies to eligible employees in participating teams at level 70 and below, in Canada, India, the United Kingdom and the United States.
- "Some organizations are providing the Voluntary Exit Offer, and some are not."
- Eligible employees get an email with their offer details.

Press coverage adds detail the blog post does not give. Euronews puts the number of eligible employees at about 60,000. Other outlets report that employees decide in December and would leave on December 31, and that terms resemble standard severance. Reports differ on the exact eligibility cutoff, so anyone affected should rely on their own offer email.

Microsoft's post does not mention AI. Some coverage frames the offer against AI spending, but that link is the press's reading, not something Microsoft stated. This article does not claim AI caused it. It is a story about what a lot of experienced people may do next.

## What to do before you decide

1. **Write your offer in one sentence.** "I help mid-size logistics firms migrate reporting to the cloud" beats "senior program manager seeking opportunities."
2. **Collect proof you are allowed to share.** Public talks, certifications, personal projects. Do not publish anything covered by confidentiality agreements.
3. **Check your agreement.** Non-compete, moonlighting and intellectual property terms vary. Read yours or ask a lawyer before taking clients.
4. **Build the page and the delivery channel first.** A site where people can see your offer and contact you, plus a private space where clients get files and status.
5. **Test demand while you are still employed**, within what your contract allows. A live page and ten conversations tell you more than a spreadsheet.
